Your city is tracking every car you drive.

135 automated license plate readers blanket Santa Maria, logging 22.5 million plate reads every month. 104 of those cameras were purchased with federal COVID relief funds — and never publicly disclosed. This is what we found through public records.

Key findings

What the records revealed

Internal audits, contracts, and emails paint a picture of a surveillance program with minimal oversight and zero public transparency.

Off-duty officer searched plates with no documented reason
A June 2023 audit found an officer who searched license plates two hours after their shift ended. The officer entered the plate number itself as the "reason" for the search. No corresponding records existed in any police system — no CAD call, no case number, no report — to justify the query.
Misuse
8 plates searched in 4 minutes with reason "test"
A December 2022 audit flagged an officer who searched 8 different license plates in just 4 minutes, entering "test" as the reason for each query. The auditor was unable to verify whether these searches had any legitimate law enforcement purpose.
Misuse
33% of spot-checked searches were unverifiable
In February 2023, one-third of the searches selected for audit could not be verified as having a legitimate law enforcement purpose. The audit process itself only reviews 4 to 8 searches per quarter — negligible oversight for a system processing 22.5 million reads monthly.
Oversight failure
18-month gap between audits
No audits were conducted between Q1 2024 and Q3 2025 — an 18-month window during which the surveillance system operated with zero documented oversight. During that period, the cameras captured an estimated 400+ million plate reads with no accountability review.
Oversight failure
104 cameras purchased with COVID funds — never disclosed
104 additional ALPR cameras were purchased using federal American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) funds — money intended for COVID-19 relief. This expansion was never publicly disclosed or presented to the City Council. It was only discovered through internal emails obtained via CPRA request.
Secret expansion
Housing Authority cameras may operate on an expired contract
19 ALPR cameras are deployed at Housing Authority properties. When asked to produce the governing contract, staff could not even locate the agreement file. These cameras may be operating without a valid, current contract.
Missing contract
Second Flock contract never provided despite 3 CPRA rounds
Records indicate a second contract with Flock Safety beginning October 2024 exists. Despite three rounds of California Public Records Act requests specifically asking for all contracts, this agreement has never been provided by the city.

About this project

How we got this information

Every finding on this page is based on documents provided by the City of Santa Maria in response to requests filed under the California Public Records Act (Government Code Section 6250 et seq.).

This project exists because residents deserve to know how their city deploys surveillance technology, how it is funded, and whether meaningful safeguards exist to prevent misuse. The public record shows that, in Santa Maria, the answers are troubling.

No information on this page was obtained through unauthorized access or leaked documents. This is your government's own paperwork.

  • All data sourced from official CPRA responses
  • Audit findings quoted from city-produced audit documents
  • Contract amounts from executed agreements on file
  • Camera locations from Flock Safety dashboard exports
  • ARPA spending revealed through internal email correspondence
  • No leaked or unauthorized documents used
